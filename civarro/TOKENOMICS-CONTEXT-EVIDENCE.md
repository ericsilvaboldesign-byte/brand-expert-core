## INTERPRETATION

Analyst inference; nothing here upgrades the evidence.

1. **Tokenomics is the market context that makes Civarro's
   settlement/attribution role legible.** If tokens are the unit of AI
   cost, budgets rise monthly, and agentic work executes as long
   multi-step chains, then *which compute served which unit of work, at
   what volume, under what conditions* (the deck's own description of
   Civarro's settlement function) is the emerging economic bookkeeping
   problem of the category. The AMD deck's authors chose this quote as
   their opening slide — evidence they frame the POC as an answer to
   tokenomics, with Civarro holding the settlement end of that answer.
2. **The inference/agentic turn moves the cost problem to where the deck
   places Civarro.** Training cost is one vendor's bill; inference cost in
   agentic chains is distributed across whoever's infrastructure each hop
   runs on. Distributed cost is what makes attribution and settlement a
   real problem rather than an accounting detail.
3. **"Tokenomics" is established third-party vocabulary.** A running
   editorial series at diginomica plus wide trade-press use means the term
   needs no explanation to Civarro's likely audience and carries no
   partner-specific baggage.
4. **The vendor-side angle of the quote matters.** The article's point is
   that tokenomics squeezes *vendors*, not only end users — so the cost
   problem exists on the operator/vendor side of the market, not only the
   buyer side. Boundary of this inference: that the problem exists there
   does not establish that operators or workload owners would *pay* for
   attribution/settlement services, nor that they are Civarro's audience
   or customer. Willingness to pay and the paying customer are **UNKNOWN /
   NOT ESTABLISHED** in all accessed evidence.

## WEBSITE IMPLICATION

Strategy input, not approved copy; contingent on the verification items.

- **A safe "why now" narrative exists in public evidence.** Unlike the POC
  and company names (blocked), the market context — inference overtaking
  training, agentic chains multiplying token consumption, cost-per-token
  as the enterprise concern — is publicly documented by third-party press
  and citable. A website "why now" section can stand on this context
  without touching any unverified relationship or capability claim.
- **Terminology candidates from public usage:** *tokenomics; cost per
  token; unit of work; inference era; agentic workloads.* "Unit of work"
  bridges naturally from the market language to the deck's description of
  Civarro's settlement function.
- **Narrative shape:** market context (public, citable) → the coordination
  and attribution problem it creates (interpretation, arguable) →
  Civarro's role (only what verification clears). This layering lets the
  website be concrete about the problem while remaining strictly bounded
  about the product.
- **Data-point discipline:** if the 35-quadrillion / 160x / 60% figures
  are used, they must be attributed to AMD's keynote as first-party
  claims (e.g., "AMD reports…"), never presented as independent
  measurements — and never in a way that implies AMD endorses Civarro.
