# brand-expert-core — Phase 1: Verified Source Corpus

Phase 1 answers one question:

> **What professional branding / brand design knowledge is actually supported by
> which real sources, at what level of authority, and with what limits?**

It is a knowledge-acquisition and source-verification deliverable. There is no
code here. The corpus is two documents:

| Document | What it contains |
|----------|------------------|
| [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md) | The verified source register: every source that backs a claim in this corpus, with full citation, the evidence used to verify it exists as described, its authority level, and its known limits. |
| [`KNOWLEDGE-MAP.md`](KNOWLEDGE-MAP.md) | The substance: branding knowledge organized by domain, each claim mapped to the sources that support it, graded by authority, with limits and live contradictions stated rather than smoothed over. |

## How sources were verified

Every source in the register was checked against a real-world reference —
publisher catalog, journal record / DOI resolver, WorldCat or library records —
for exact title, authors, publisher/venue, and year. Identifiers (ISBN-13, DOI)
are recorded only where read from such a reference, never from memory. Each
entry lists the verification URL used. Claims about a source's *limits* cite
published critiques where they exist and are marked as analyst judgment where
they do not.

## Authority levels

The success criterion requires grading, so the corpus uses five levels. A grade
describes **the kind of warrant a source provides**, not its usefulness:

| Level | Meaning | Warrant |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **A1** | Peer-reviewed empirical research | Data: experiments, meta-analyses, large-N studies in refereed journals |
| **A2** | Peer-reviewed / scholarly conceptual work | Theory that survived academic scrutiny and decades of citation — coherent and teachable, but not itself a tested finding |
| **B** | Industry-empirical | Large real-world datasets analyzed rigorously outside journal peer review (e.g., IPA effectiveness databank, Ehrenberg-Bass corpus) — powerful but with documented selection limits |
| **C** | Practitioner canon | Professional consensus from leading practitioners; case-supported, no controlled evidence — reliable about *craft and process*, unreliable as *causal claims* |
| **D** | Popular / opinion | Influential ideas with essentially no evidence base — treated as hypotheses, never as knowledge |

A source can carry different weight for different claims; the knowledge map
grades **claims**, the register grades **sources** by their dominant mode.

## The short answer

What the field actually *knows* (A1/B): how brands grow (penetration beats
loyalty; double jeopardy; mental and physical availability), that advertising's
sales effects are real but conditional, that fluency and familiarity drive
liking of brand assets, and a handful of design findings (logo elaborateness
and naturalness, sound symbolism in naming). What it *teaches as frameworks*
(A2): brand equity models, identity systems, cultural branding. What it
*practices as craft* (C): identity-design process, positioning discipline,
radical differentiation. What it *repeats without evidence* (D): purpose as a
growth driver, story formulas as proven persuasion. The map documents each,
with the contradictions between them left visible — most importantly
differentiation (Ries/Trout, Neumeier) versus distinctiveness (Sharp,
Romaniuk), which the field has not resolved.
