# Branding / Rebranding Case-Source Ecosystem

Status: CURATED DISCOVERY + CRITIQUE LAYER — NOT PRIMARY EVIDENCE BY DEFAULT

Purpose: strengthen Brand Expert Core with a professional case-review layer focused on branding and rebranding, especially before/after context, strategic rationale, critique, system breadth, and long-term follow-up.

## Governing distinction

This corpus needs more than design-system repos.

Use three layers together:

1. **PRIMARY CASE SOURCE** — agency/client case study, official guidelines, official rollout material.
2. **EDITORIAL / CRITICAL SOURCE** — independent or semi-independent reporting, critique, comparison, interviews, before/after context.
3. **OBSERVED VISUAL EVIDENCE** — actual identity assets and applications inspected directly.

No editorial source below should silently replace the primary agency/client source when that source is obtainable.

## PRIORITY A — core editorial/critical sources

### Brand New / UnderConsideration

Role: HIGH-VALUE EDITORIAL CRITIQUE + CASE DISCOVERY.

Why it matters:
- extensive archive of logo, identity and branding projects;
- explicit before/after framing;
- separates Reviewed / Noted / Spotted / Announced / Revisited;
- strong current coverage, including 2026 projects;
- includes practitioner opinion and community response rather than only agency self-description.

Use for:
- discovering rebrands worth studying;
- understanding what changed from prior identity;
- external critique of logo/system decisions;
- identifying controversial or unusually effective-looking changes;
- building temporal follow-up cases when a project is revisited.

Boundary:
Brand New is professional editorial opinion, not empirical proof of business effectiveness. Treat its judgments as critique, not fact.

### Brandemia

Role: SPANISH-LANGUAGE / IBERO-AMERICAN EDITORIAL + CASE DISCOVERY.

Why it matters:
- sustained coverage of branding, rebranding, identity, naming, verbal identity, strategy and corporate culture;
- strong Spanish-language context and useful Latin American / Spanish case coverage;
- publishes annual/barometer-style roundups and explicit reactions to notable rebrands.

Use for:
- Ibero-American case discovery;
- Spanish-language professional discourse;
- comparing strategy, verbal identity and visual identity coverage;
- avoiding an exclusively US/UK design canon.

Boundary:
Editorial interpretation does not substitute for original case documentation or independent outcome evidence.

### Design Week

Role: PROFESSIONAL DESIGN JOURNALISM / INTERVIEW / RETROSPECTIVE.

Why it matters:
- long-running Brand Identity archive;
- frequently interviews studios and clients about brief, positioning and design decisions;
- can provide implementation detail absent from agency case pages;
- especially valuable when it revisits projects years later.

High-value pattern:
Longitudinal follow-up. Example: its 2025 retrospective on FutureBrand's 2015 Merck rebrand includes original strategic problem, design rationale, internal adoption and ten-year reflection.

Boundary:
Separate reported client/agency claims from independently verified outcomes. A retrospective can be richer than a launch case study without becoming causal proof.

### BP&O — Branding, Packaging and Opinion

Role: SPECIALIST EDITORIAL CRITIQUE.

Why it matters:
- close visual analysis of identity and packaging systems;
- useful for heritage brands, redesigns, packaging and asset continuity;
- often examines whether a refresh amplifies existing brand equities rather than replacing them.

Use for:
- design criticism;
- asset preservation/evolution;
- packaging-system analysis;
- comparing radical rebrand vs disciplined refresh.

Boundary:
Opinion/criticism, not effectiveness evidence.

## PRIORITY B — strong professional discovery / reporting sources

### It's Nice That

Role: DESIGN JOURNALISM + DESIGNER INTERVIEWS + CONTEMPORARY PRACTICE.

Useful for:
- detailed interviews with designers;
- process and cultural context;
- recent rebrands and emerging studios;
- non-canonical and geographically broader work;
- critical essays about sameness, commercial design and category conventions.

Particularly useful because it can expose the reasoning between strategy and formal decisions rather than only showing polished final assets.

Boundary:
Some pieces are features, sponsored collaborations or studio-led descriptions. Classify article type before use.

### Creative Boom

Role: CONTEMPORARY BRANDING DISCOVERY / INTERVIEW / NEWS.

Useful for:
- current identity and rebrand discovery;
- studio interviews;
- visual examples;
- broad mix of established and smaller practices.

Boundary:
Use as a lead and contextual source. Validate key strategic/design claims against primary studio/client material when available.

### The Brand Identity

Role: VISUAL CASE DISCOVERY + STUDIO INTERVIEW.

Useful for:
- system-rich contemporary projects;
- many project pages expose multiple identity dimensions (logo, digital, motion, packaging, type, etc.);
- interviews can reveal system logic and studio reasoning;
- stronger coverage of emerging/non-canonical studios than a canon-only corpus.

Boundary:
Project descriptions often rely heavily on studio-supplied rationale. Keep SOURCE-STATED INTENT separate from OBSERVATION and OUR INFERENCE.

### Identity Designed

Role: CURATED IDENTITY CASE ARCHIVE.

Useful for:
- cases presented across multiple applications;
- substantial Latin American / Mexican design representation through studios such as Anagrama and Futura;
- naming, identity, packaging, environments and applied systems;
- older cases useful for longevity comparison.

Boundary:
Much case copy is supplied by or closely aligned with the design studio. Treat it as primary-practice testimony when provenance is clear, not independent evaluation.

## CASE-SELECTION IMPLICATION

Do not build the training corpus around whatever is easiest to download.

Target a portfolio of branding/rebranding problems, including:

- corporate transformation;
- heritage refresh;
- radical repositioning;
- merger / architecture consolidation;
- consumer packaging;
- B2B / technology;
- cultural institution;
- place / destination;
- digital-native product;
- brand extension / sub-brand system;
- identity with strong motion/dynamic behavior;
- identity where preserving existing assets was the central move;
- identity where the previous brand had to be deliberately broken from.

Include geographic diversity, especially Latin America, continental Europe, Asia, Africa and the Middle East where credible source coverage exists.

## RECOMMENDED CASE RECORD

For every serious rebrand training case, preserve five distinct records:

### 1. BEFORE STATE
What existed before: mark, category codes, architecture, recognition assets, known problem.

### 2. PRIMARY STATED PROBLEM / STRATEGY
What client/agency says it was trying to change.

### 3. OBSERVED NEW SYSTEM
What is actually visible in the new identity across components/applications.

### 4. EDITORIAL / CRITICAL RECEPTION
What informed third-party design sources notice, praise, question or criticize.

### 5. LATER EVIDENCE
If obtainable: subsequent adoption, continuity, reversal, iteration, rollout problems, recognition research, or long-term use.

Never collapse these layers into one success story.

---

## WHY THIS CHANGES THE EXPERT CORE

Technical design systems such as Carbon remain valuable for implementation and digital brand behavior, but they are not enough to teach branding judgment.

The core branding/rebranding corpus should teach the model to reason about:
- why a brand changed;
- what should have been preserved;
- what strategic shift the identity was asked to express;
- how much change was actually necessary;
- what system choices carried that shift;
- how external professional critics interpreted the work;
- what cannot be inferred about effectiveness from launch imagery alone.

This editorial/critical layer complements — and never replaces — primary visual evidence.
