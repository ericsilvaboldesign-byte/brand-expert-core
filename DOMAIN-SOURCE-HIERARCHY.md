# Domain Source Hierarchy

Status: CURATED SOURCE MAP — NOT METHODOLOGY

Purpose: organize the Brand Expert Core by professional domain while preventing agency blogs, galleries, research papers, practitioner frameworks and empirical evidence from being treated as interchangeable authorities.

## 1. Brand strategy / positioning

### Governing sources
Use original established strategy/equity/positioning literature already present in the corpus (e.g. Keller, Kapferer, Aaker and other directly verified sources) for construct definitions and disagreements.

### Professional-practice layer
**Dexa Now** — useful as current agency practice on connecting business context, positioning, value proposition, verbal/visual identity, digital experience and governance. Its own article explicitly says there is no single model that fits every organization.

**Ramotion** — broad professional commentary and case/discovery source across brand strategy, visual identity, verbal identity, web, product and design systems.

Boundary: neither Dexa nor Ramotion is a canonical authority for the definition of positioning or brand strategy. Use them to observe current practice and operational translation, not to override source-backed theory.

## 2. Brand personality

### Governing construct
Use Jennifer Aaker (1997) only for the construct/scale it actually establishes, with the existing Azoulay & Kapferer critique and cultural/construct limits preserved.

### Visual-translation research
**Andrade, Morais & de Lima (2024), The Personality of Visual Elements** — ORIGINAL RESEARCH, directly accessible. The paper proposes and tests a framework linking Aaker's five personality dimensions with combinations of color, typography and shape.

Important evidence boundaries:
- sample N=127;
- predominantly Brazilian and Portuguese participants;
- ten fictitious visual identities;
- visual stimulus presented through branded merchandising mockups;
- authors describe results as preliminary;
- framework is intentionally generalist;
- it does not prove that personality should mechanically determine visual identity;
- it does not establish universal color/type/shape meanings across cultures, categories or contexts;
- it inherits the conceptual limits of Aaker's five-dimension framework.

Professional use: useful evidence that visual elements can influence perceived personality under tested conditions; not a lookup table for automatic design decisions.

## 3. Verbal identity

### Professional-practice sources
**Verbal Identity / Chris West / Strong Language** — practitioner source for creating and codifying commercially usable brand voice. The consultancy positions verbal identity as the way a brand uses language to become recognizable and stand for something, and publishes a practical framework through *Strong Language*.

**Saboteur Studio** — useful alternative professional method: begin with strong writing that already represents the brand, then codify it into usable techniques rather than beginning from generic adjectives.

Boundary: these are professional methodologies, not empirical laws. Preserve the Item-10 lesson: there is no source-established universal `personality → voice → tone` pipeline merely because practitioners often connect those constructs.

## 4. Dynamic / generative identity

Use `DYNAMIC-GENERATIVE-IDENTITY-SOURCES.md`.

Keep responsive, adaptive, dynamic and generative as overlapping professional vocabularies unless a specific source defines them. Do not freeze them into a universal taxonomy.

Priority: peer-reviewed responsive/adaptive-mark research + specialist practice such as Irene van Nes and Patrik Hübner, with technical implementation treated separately from effectiveness evidence.

## 5. Distinctive assets

### Governing evidence
**Ehrenberg-Bass / Jenni Romaniuk** remains primary. Distinctive assets are non-brand-name identity elements that become linked to the brand in memory through marketing activity; Fame and Uniqueness are empirical concepts, not synonyms for aesthetic originality.

### Secondary/professional tools
**Distinctive BAT** and similar tools may be useful for operationalization, visualization or practitioner education only after their specific methodology is verified.

Boundary: do not substitute a commercial diagnostic platform for original Ehrenberg-Bass research. A recurring visual feature seen in a case study may be a POTENTIAL BRAND IDENTIFIER; visual inspection alone cannot establish Fame or Uniqueness.

## 6. Typography

Use existing `TYPOGRAPHY.md` plus **Fonts In Use** as an observational archive of typography in actual applications.

Boundary: phrases such as “typography is the visual voice of the brand” are practitioner metaphors, not empirical definitions. Do not attach unsupported percentages to typography's role in contemporary branding.

## 7. Color / imagery / graphic language

Use `COLOR-IMAGERY.md` for source-backed findings.

Use Brandemia, Visual Journal, The Brand Identity, Identity Designed, DIELINE and other curated case sources for observation/discovery.

Boundary: a curated gallery does not establish semiotic meaning, consumer perception, asset distinctiveness or effectiveness. Trace psychological claims to empirical evidence and cultural/semiotic claims to legitimate theory.

## 8. Semiotics

Use `SEMIOTICS.md`.

Do not treat Visual Journal or any inspiration gallery as a semiotic authority. Galleries provide visual material; semiotic analysis requires a credible theoretical basis and explicit separation of:
- observation;
- cultural/semiotic interpretation;
- strategic inference.

## 9. Digital design systems

### Primary implementation sources
Prefer official systems and documentation such as IBM Carbon, Shopify Polaris, Atlassian Design System, Material Design and other client-owned systems where directly accessible.

### Secondary discovery
UXPin's design-system roundups are useful for locating mature systems and comparing public documentation, not as the authority for what each system actually specifies.

Boundary: design systems operationalize product/UI consistency and governance. They are not synonymous with brand identity and should not determine brand strategy.

## 10. Rebranding criticism / case discovery

Use `BRANDING-REBRANDING-CASE-ECOSYSTEM.md`.

High-value editorial sources include Brand New / UnderConsideration, Brandemia, Design Week, BP&O, It's Nice That and comparable sources.

Required case stack:
1. primary agency/client source;
2. independent editorial/critical source where available;
3. directly observed visual evidence;
4. later evidence/follow-up when possible.

Editorial criticism is not empirical proof, but it is valuable because it introduces before/after context, disagreement and professional judgment missing from agency self-report.

## 11. Visual-case discovery

Use `BRAND-IDENTITY-GALLERIES.md`.

The Brand Identity, Identity Designed, Rebrand Gallery, WBDS, Behance, Visual Journal, Fonts In Use and similar archives are discovery/observation sources. They must not become hidden methodology.

## 12. Motion / creative coding / computational brand expression

Use `MOTION-CREATIVE-CODING-SOURCES.md`.

Primary technical authority: official GSAP, three.js, browser/platform and library documentation.

Professional laboratory: Codrops and comparable sources with working demos/code.

Execution radar: Awwwards, CSS Design Awards, Refs.Gallery, FreeFrontend and similar galleries.

Boundary: technical sophistication and award recognition do not establish brand distinctiveness, usability or effectiveness.

## Governing source rule

For every domain ask:

**What kind of question am I answering?**

- If it is a construct-definition question → prefer original theory/research.
- If it is an empirical-effect question → require empirical evidence.
- If it is a professional-process question → original practitioner/agency sources may govern practice claims.
- If it is a technical-implementation question → official technical documentation governs capability.
- If it is a contemporary-execution question → galleries/awards may supply observation.
- If it is a case-interpretation question → separate agency-stated intent, observed evidence, editorial critique and our inference.

No source earns authority outside the question it is actually capable of answering.
