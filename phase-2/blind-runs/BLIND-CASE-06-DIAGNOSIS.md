## A. OBSERVATION

**Symbol.** A new-looking circular device: inside a circle (or as a circular cut form), a white sweeping shape resembling an orbital path or stylized arrow curving up and to the right, with a sharp notch/point at its lower right. It reads variously as an orbit, a "D" in motion, an arrowhead, or an abstract swoosh-in-circle. It appears: locked left of the wordmark on signage; alone at architectural scale (acrylic cube sign); embroidered small on garment chests; as a moulded/printed tab on footwear; alone on blue tiles.

**Wordmark.** "DECATHLON" in heavy white geometric-grotesque caps with subtle customizations (angled cuts visible in some letters; a compact, engineered feel). Always white on blue in the evidence.

**Color.** One saturated blue dominates everything — fascia, tiles, garment, sign. The acrylic sign introduces a translucent, luminous rendering of the same blue. White is the only counter-color; the shoe introduces neutral product tones.

**Applications.** Big-box exterior signage; small-format urban signage (symbol-only); product branding at garment and footwear scale; brand-block graphics.

**Constants:** the blue; the white symbol; the white caps wordmark; symbol-left lockup.

**Variables:** symbol used with or without wordmark; scale (shoe tab → building); material rendering (paint, embroidery, moulding, translucent acrylic).

**Hierarchy:** color field first at distance, symbol second, wordmark third; on products, the symbol alone represents the brand.

## B. SYSTEM LOGIC

1. **Compression to a symbol.** The system's visible center of gravity is a new circular mark that can stand alone — on a shoe, a chest, a street sign — where a ten-letter wordmark cannot. The evidence repeatedly demonstrates symbol-without-wordmark usage, which implies the core mechanism: give a long-named retailer a compact identifier for product and small-format contexts. INTERPRETATION strongly supported by observed usage.
2. **Monolithic single-color world.** One blue everywhere, one white mark: a maximal-consistency, minimal-vocabulary system built for global scale and franchise/vendor execution (INTERPRETATION; the chromatic uniformity is observed).
3. **Retail-to-product-brand bridge.** Chest embroidery and shoe tabs are product-brand behaviors (apparel-label codes), not retailer behaviors; the system equips the company to brand merchandise the way sports labels do (INTERPRETATION from application types).
4. **Format elasticity of retail presence.** The pairing of big-box fascia and a small elegant urban sign cube suggests the identity must serve both warehouse-scale suburban retail and compact city-center formats. INTERPRETATION from the two signage contexts shown.
5. **Motion implication.** The symbol's swept form implies direction/rotation and would lend itself to rotational motion behavior; no motion evidence exists (plausibility only).

## D. CREATIVE JUDGMENT

One-sentence system diagnosis: a heavy-infrastructure rebrand whose one decisive move — a compact, product-ready circular symbol added to a preserved blue — is strategically sensible and operationally motivated, but whose symbol enters the most crowded archetype in sport (dynamic swoosh-forms) and whose system depth cannot be judged from this thin package.

- **Specificity — the open question.** The blue-field/white-caps retail dress is category-generic (big-box retail worldwide). The new symbol is where specificity must live, and it is a hybrid of familiar sports-mark tropes: circular containment, upward sweep, notched arrow. It is competently distinct in silhouette — the notch and the orbit reading give it a graspable feature — but it sits in the same formal family as countless sport/motion marks. Its ownability will depend on saturation of use rather than on formal originality (judgment, not measurement).
- **Coherence — high, by economy.** With effectively two elements (blue, white mark) the system cannot be incoherent; every shown application agrees. This is coherence by reduction rather than by orchestration — appropriate for the apparent scale problem.
- **Range — barely evidenced.** Signage and product marking are shown; nothing else. No campaign expression, no typography-in-use, no digital, no photography system, no secondary palette. Whether the identity has an expressive register at all is unknowable from the package.
- **Operationality — the system's evident purpose and likely strength.** A one-color, one-symbol system with a robust silhouette is close to optimal for global franchise execution: embroidery, moulding, signage fabrication, low-cost print. The symbol demonstrably survives four materials and five scales in evidence. The long wordmark remains a liability at small scale — which the symbol exists to solve (INTERPRETATION).
- **Craft — competent at mark level, unassessable at system level.** The symbol's curves and notch are cleanly resolved; the wordmark's customization is restrained. The acrylic sign shows aspirational fabrication quality, though it appears rendered.
- **Generative power — low-to-moderate on evidence.** The symbol + blue can generate placements but not much language: no pattern logic, graphic device, or compositional grammar is shown. As evidenced, this is an emblem system, not a design system — which may be the correct scope for the problem, but limits what the identity itself can say.

## E. RISKS / FAILURE MODES

- **Category camouflage at both layers.** Blue big-box retail dress is globally generic; dynamic circular sports marks are a saturated archetype. The combination (this blue + this symbol + this name) must do all the attribution work; each ingredient alone attributes weakly. This is the system's principal risk (CATEGORY CAMOUFLAGE, partial).
- **Symbol familiarity / near-resemblance.** Swoosh-adjacent forms invite comparison and possible confusion with established sport marks in memory; formal proximity to famous marks is a known hazard for new sports symbols (raised as professional judgment; no specific conflict is asserted).
- **Equity transition risk.** If the previous identity's recognition lived in the wordmark treatment (undetermined here), a symbol-led future spends years teaching a new mark; the package shows no transitional devices. UNKNOWN-dependent risk.
- **Emblem-only thinness.** With no shown graphic language, campaigns and digital surfaces will need vocabulary the evidence doesn't contain — risk of every application being "blue field + logo" (ONE-TRICK / NO-VARIABLES tendency as evidenced; possibly resolved in unshown guidelines).
- **Mockup share.** The most seductive image (translucent acrylic cube) appears rendered; fabrication reality unverified.
- **Package contamination (evaluation-level, already flagged).** The visible agency credit and rebrand date compromise blind validity; additionally, an "@agency" credit frame is itself promotional material, so the package partly consists of announcement graphics rather than neutral evidence — AGENCY-COPY-adjacent material was treated as observation-of-artifact only.
