## A. OBSERVATION

Register: OBSERVATION unless marked otherwise.

**Wordmark.** "FANTA" in heavy white uppercase letterforms with irregular, hand-cut contours — edges look scissor-cut rather than drawn with geometric precision; letters bounce off a shared baseline and rotate slightly; the counters and cuts differ letter to letter. An ® is attached. On the "zero sugar" can the sub-descriptor is set in a matching chunky lowercase.

**Blue field device.** The wordmark sits on a deep ultramarine/cobalt blue angular field that descends from the can's top edge as an irregular, faceted V/shard shape — its silhouette is cut at angles that echo the wordmark's cut-paper character. The white letters overlap and slightly break the field's edges. This unit (white cut wordmark + blue faceted field) repeats identically in structure on every can, bottle, and carton shown.

**Flavor tab.** A small rectangular tab in a darker shade of the flavor color, carrying the flavor name in white lowercase ("orange", "grape", "lemon", "melon", "mango", "cherry", "raspberry", "pineapple", "lime", "blackcurrant"), tucked against the lower-left edge of the blue field.

**Flavor illustration.** Flat two-tone vector fruit (slice, bunch, or whole fruit) with stylized juice-splash droplets bursting from beneath the blue field's point. No photography, no gloss, no 3D fruit rendering. Droplet/splash shapes recur as freestanding graphic elements in the hero image and enlarged as carton patterns.

**Color behavior.** Each can body is a single flat, saturated flavor color (orange, yellow, pink, red, purple, greens, blue). The blue field and white wordmark are constant across all flavors. Bottle caps are the same blue; a cap medallion carries a white "F" on blue. The presentation background is the same blue, used as a brand environment.

**Secondary typography.** Descriptor lines ("ORANGE SODA NATURALLY FLAVORED") in blue caps in a rounded, simple sans; regulatory/nutrition marks; "New Irresistible Taste" in a white speech-bubble-like callout; carton side panels reuse "RECYCLE ME — SEE YOU AGAIN SOON!" in the brand voice.

**Constants across the package:** the cut-paper wordmark; the blue faceted field; blue as the fixed anchor color; the flavor-tab construction; the flat splash-and-fruit illustration grammar; lowercase flavor naming; the rounded descriptor sans.

**Variables:** can/carton body color per flavor; the fruit illustrated; splash color mixes (always derived from the flavor palette plus white/cream); scale of the splash pattern (small on cans, enlarged to near-abstraction on cartons); presence of functional callouts (zero sugar, New Irresistible Taste).

**Hierarchy:** wordmark unit first, flavor color second, fruit/flavor identification third, descriptor copy last. The hierarchy is identical across every SKU shown.

**Verbal markers:** playful, spoken-register copy ("See you again soon!") — limited evidence.

## B. SYSTEM LOGIC

Best-supported explanation (OBSERVATION + INTERPRETATION, labeled):

1. **Fixed-masthead architecture over variable flavor fields.** The system's core mechanism is a rigid, high-contrast brand unit (white cut wordmark on blue faceted field) that never changes, floated over a fully variable flavor layer (body color + fruit illustration + tab). Recognition is designed to survive any flavor color because the identifier is chromatically independent of the variant. This is directly observed across ~12 SKUs.
2. **Blue as the portfolio glue.** In a fruit-flavored category where variant color must communicate flavor, the constant blue — an unexpected hue for fruit soda — functions as the cross-range attribution anchor, extended beyond pack into the presentation environment (backgrounds, caps, cap medallion). INTERPRETATION on intent; the consistency itself is observed.
3. **One graphic grammar, one voice, many SKUs.** Fruit is always flat vector; juice is always the same droplet-splash vocabulary; flavor names are always lowercase in a tab. The system reads as a kit of parts an ordinary production team could apply to a new flavor without new creative decisions (INTERPRETATION as to governance intent).
4. **Form language coherence.** The cut-paper irregularity is carried through consistently: wordmark contours, the faceted blue field, and the angular splash shapes share one "cut/torn" formal DNA, so the playfulness is systemic rather than applied decoration.
5. **Scalable pattern behavior.** The splash/droplet elements operate at three observed scales — detail on can, enlarged pattern on carton, freestanding sticker-like elements in hero compositions — indicating a variable-scale graphic asset rather than a fixed illustration.
6. **Monolithic architecture with functional overlays.** Variants (zero sugar) are handled inside the same masthead unit rather than by a separate sub-design; promotional/functional messages get contained callout shapes that do not disturb the core unit.

## D. CREATIVE JUDGMENT

One-sentence system diagnosis: a disciplined, production-friendly portfolio system whose recognition burden is deliberately concentrated in one fixed wordmark-plus-blue unit, executed with a coherent cut-paper form language — strong on architecture and governance, more conventional at the level of individual ingredients.

- **Specificity — good at the unit level, weaker at the ingredient level.** Taken separately, flat fruit vectors, juice splashes, and flavor-colored cans are category stock. The specific combination — irregular cut white letters breaking a faceted blue shard, on any flavor color — is a distinctive construction that a competitor could not adopt without visibly imitating. The blue-on-fruit-color masthead is the single most ownable move in evidence. The splash grammar alone is low-specificity.
- **Coherence — strong.** One formal idea (cut/torn paper irregularity) runs through wordmark, field, splashes, and even the flavor tab's angled placement. Hierarchy, tab construction, descriptor typography, and illustration style are identical across all ~12 SKUs and three formats shown. This is a genuinely systemic identity, not a styled range.
- **Range — strong within the shown domain, unproven outside it.** The system demonstrably absorbs at least ten flavor colors, cans, PET bottles, caps, and cartons, plus a zero-sugar variant, without strain — real range evidence for a packaging system. Nothing is shown beyond packaging and staged brand environments: no advertising, digital, motion, or retail context. The expressive ceiling (what a campaign looks like when it can't just be cans on blue) is undemonstrated.
- **Operationality — the system's apparent strength.** The kit-of-parts logic (fixed masthead + flavor color + one illustration style + tab) is exactly what large licensed/bottler networks can execute consistently. Flat vector art survives cheap printing, small formats, and shrink film far better than photographic fruit. Two caveats: all evidence is idealized 3D rendering, not shelf photography — mockup beauty is not operational proof; and some flavor-tab colorways (dark tab on saturated body) plus small blue descriptor text on mid-tone bodies would need legibility verification at real size and distance.
- **Craft — controlled within its register.** The wordmark's irregularity is consistent and deliberate (repeat letters behave identically across SKUs, so the "hand-cut" quality is engineered, not sloppy). Splash shapes are confidently drawn; color relationships between body, tab, fruit, and splashes are managed per flavor. The renders are polished — which is presentation craft, not system craft, and is discounted accordingly.
- **Generative power — moderate-to-good.** The cut/splash vocabulary demonstrably generates pattern (cartons), stickers/badges (hero image), and could plausibly generate motion (cut shapes animating) and environments — plausibility, not evidence. The core idea is an architecture plus a form language, which generates decisions for new SKUs well; whether it generates communication beyond packaging is unshown.

## E. RISKS / FAILURE MODES

- **Ingredient genericity (partial CATEGORY CAMOUFLAGE).** Remove the wordmark unit and the remaining layer — flavor-colored can, flat fruit, splash — could belong to several fruit-drink brands, including private label. Attribution rides almost entirely on the masthead unit and the blue. That concentration is a deliberate architecture, but it makes the system fragile anywhere the masthead is cropped, miniaturized, or absent.
- **Trend exposure.** The chunky, bouncing, cut-out letter aesthetic and flat "sticker" illustration sit inside a current wave of playful FMCG redesigns. The architecture would survive a future retune of the letterforms, so exposure is partial (TREND-AS-STRATEGY risk at the styling layer, not the structural layer).
- **Mockup-only evidence (HERO-SHOT risk, unresolved).** Every image is a controlled render on brand-blue ground. On a real shelf the background is competitive noise, not brand blue; lighting, curvature, and shrink-sleeve distortion are untested in this package. The system's logic suggests it should survive shelf reality, but the package does not prove it.
- **Legibility/accessibility.** Small white lowercase flavor names on mid-value tabs, and blue descriptor text on saturated mid-tone bodies (orange, green), need contrast verification at actual print size; the bouncing wordmark trades some instant legibility for character (acceptable for a famous name, riskier for unfamiliar-market shoppers — INFERENCE).
- **Governance of playfulness.** Irregular letterforms and free-floating splash elements invite drift when local markets or promo partners produce their own assets; without visible rules (none supplied), the "engineered messiness" could degrade into actual messiness (GOVERNANCE VACUUM risk — not observed, but undefended in evidence).
- **Cultural/regulatory variation.** Flavor color conventions and fruit iconography read differently across markets (e.g., grape purple, blackcurrant), and "naturally flavored" copy is jurisdiction-specific; the package shows English-language executions only. UNIVERSAL SYMBOLISM must not be assumed.
- **Latent one-trick risk — low.** The splash motif repeats everywhere; at portfolio scale that is coherence, but communications built solely on "splash + can" would exhaust quickly.

No CRITICAL failure modes are observable within the supplied material itself.
