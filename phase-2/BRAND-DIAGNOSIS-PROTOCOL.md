# Brand Expert Core — Brand Diagnosis Protocol

Status: PHASE 2 OPERATIONAL SYNTHESIS

Purpose: provide a repeatable way to inspect a brand or identity system without confusing observation, taste, strategy, and effectiveness.

## 1. Inputs

Use only what is actually available:

- business / brand context;
- current strategy or owner decisions;
- existing identity assets;
- real touchpoints;
- competitor/category evidence;
- primary case/source material;
- research or measured brand data where available.

List critical absences before diagnosing if they materially constrain the answer.

## 2. Diagnosis sequence

### STEP 1 — State the actual problem

Do not begin with aesthetic critique.

Write one sentence:

`The system needs to ______ while preserving / avoiding ______.`

Examples of problem types from the training corpus include equity-preserving simplification, architecture unification, heritage revival, challenger expression, community governance, multilingual parity, or computational expression.

### STEP 2 — Inventory observable assets

Record without interpretation:

- mark / wordmark / symbol;
- typography;
- color;
- layout / grid;
- graphic devices / pattern;
- photography / illustration / image behavior;
- iconography;
- motion;
- product/UI behavior;
- physical/environmental applications;
- verbal markers where relevant.

For each asset note:

`PRESENT / ABSENT / VARIABLE / UNKNOWN`

and whether it is directly observed or source-described only.

### STEP 3 — Identify constants and variables

Ask:

- What remains recognizably stable across touchpoints?
- What is allowed to change?
- What rules control variation?
- Is variation systematic or merely stylistic inconsistency?

This is the core system-logic test.

### STEP 4 — Identify inherited equity

For an existing brand, separate:

- assets clearly retained;
- assets evolved;
- assets discarded;
- newly introduced identifiers.

Do not call any element a measured distinctive asset unless Fame/Uniqueness or equivalent evidence exists. Use `candidate identifier` or `inherited recognizable asset` when measurement is unavailable.

### STEP 5 — Category relationship

Compare with the relevant category set.

Classify visible choices as:

- category convention;
- common cross-category convention;
- uncommon but intelligible;
- potentially proprietary;
- category-breaking.

Then ask whether the choice helps fluency, differentiation, attribution, or simply trend conformity.

### STEP 6 — Strategic translation

For each major design mechanism, ask:

1. What strategic requirement could this serve?
2. Is that connection source-stated, directly observable, or our inference?
3. Does the mechanism solve more than one problem?
4. Could a competitor use the same mechanism with equal plausibility?
5. Does the mechanism scale beyond the hero application?

If the answer to #4 is yes and #5 is weak, the idea is likely generic or decorative.

### STEP 7 — Range and governance

Stress-test the system across:

- small / large scale;
- static / motion;
- product / marketing;
- dense information / expressive campaign;
- light / dark or varied backgrounds;
- different languages/scripts where relevant;
- internal teams / external partners;
- low-budget / high-production contexts.

A strong identity system should have enough rules to remain attributable without requiring art-director heroics on every output.

### STEP 8 — Craft quality

Evaluate separately from strategy:

- typographic control;
- compositional hierarchy;
- color relationships;
- image direction;
- reproduction quality;
- motion timing/behavior;
- digital performance/accessibility where relevant;
- physical production logic.

Do not excuse weak craft because the concept is clever.

### STEP 9 — Creative judgment

Judge the overall system against six questions:

1. Is it recognizable enough to accumulate memory?
2. Does it have enough range to remain alive?
3. Does it avoid obvious category cliché unless strategically useful?
4. Is the central mechanism deeper than a one-shot metaphor?
5. Can ordinary teams use it coherently?
6. Does it feel appropriate to the actual brand rather than to contemporary design culture in general?

These are professional judgment questions, not empirically validated scores.

### STEP 10 — Effectiveness boundary

Separate:

- DESIGN QUALITY — professional judgment;
- ATTRIBUTION / DISTINCTIVENESS — requires brand-memory evidence for strong claims;
- USABILITY / ACCESSIBILITY — can be evaluated with relevant methods;
- BUSINESS EFFECTIVENESS — requires outcome evidence;
- CULTURAL RECEPTION — requires relevant audience/context evidence.

Do not infer the latter four merely from visual quality.

## 3. Required diagnosis output

### VERDICT
`APPROVE / REVISE / REJECT` when evaluating a proposed direction, or a one-sentence system diagnosis for an existing identity.

### WHAT IS WORKING
Only the most consequential strengths.

### WHAT IS NOT WORKING
Problems tied to strategic/system consequences, not personal taste.

### WHY
Evidence → interpretation.

### CHANGE
Specific correction or design move.

### PRESERVE
Assets or logic that should not be casually destroyed.

### UNKNOWN
What cannot currently be concluded.

## 4. Red flags

- critique begins with `I like / I don't like`;
- generic adjectives replace observable analysis;
- every modern device is praised as innovative;
- complexity is mistaken for sophistication;
- simplicity is automatically praised;
- trend similarity is mistaken for category fit;
- literal metaphor is mistaken for a system;
- agency rationale is repeated as analysis;
- mockup beauty is mistaken for operational quality;
- a logo-only critique ignores the wider identity system.

## 5. Blind-analysis mode

For evaluation cases, hide brand name, agency, rationale, awards, and reception where possible.

Analyze visuals first. Record the diagnosis before revealing ground truth. After reveal, score what was correctly observed, what was invented, and what changed merely because of authority cues.
