# Brand Expert Core — Creative Judgment Rules

Status: PHASE 2 OPERATIONAL SYNTHESIS

Purpose: make creative evaluation explicit enough to be testable without pretending taste can be reduced to a formula.

## 1. Creative judgment is comparative

Do not ask only `Is this good?`

Ask:

- good for what problem?
- better than what alternative?
- at what cost?
- across what contexts?
- for whose use?
- with what evidence?

A direction can be visually strong and strategically wrong, strategically coherent and visually generic, or conceptually elegant and operationally unusable.

## 2. Six dimensions of professional judgment

### A. SPECIFICITY
Does the system feel particular to this brand, or could a competitor plausibly use it unchanged?

Evidence:
- proprietary/inherited assets;
- unique combinations/rules;
- connection to real brand/product/cultural material;
- avoidance of purely fashionable codes.

### B. COHERENCE
Do the parts behave as one system?

Look for:
- relationships between type, color, image, composition and motion;
- repeated rules rather than repeated decoration;
- consistent hierarchy and rhythm;
- a clear relationship between quiet and expressive modes.

### C. RANGE
Can the identity create meaningful variation without losing attribution?

A narrow system becomes monotonous; an unconstrained system becomes anonymous.

Judge both:
- recognition anchors;
- variation mechanisms.

### D. OPERATIONALITY
Can the system survive real production?

Test:
- dense documents;
- presentations;
- product UI;
- small social assets;
- low-budget outputs;
- accessibility constraints;
- external partners;
- localization;
- static fallback.

### E. CRAFT
Is execution controlled at the level appropriate to the medium?

Evaluate type, spacing, hierarchy, image selection, color behavior, motion timing, responsiveness, rendering, production quality and detail.

### F. GENERATIVE POWER
Does the core idea generate a family of decisions, or only one memorable artifact?

A strong organizing idea should help answer new design questions without requiring a new concept each time.

## 3. Distinctiveness without pseudo-measurement

In creative review, use:

- `visually specific`;
- `high attribution potential`;
- `candidate proprietary code`;
- `recognizable within the observed system`.

Do not claim market distinctiveness without memory data.

## 4. Simplicity is not a universal virtue

Praise simplification only when it improves a relevant requirement such as:

- reproduction;
- hierarchy;
- recall/recognition conditions;
- scalability;
- usability;
- architecture;
- governance.

Reject `cleaner = better` as a default evaluation rule.

Similarly, complexity can be useful when it is structured, meaningful and governable.

## 5. Novelty is not a universal virtue

Novelty can:

- create attention;
- help separate from category convention;
- unlock new expression.

But novelty can also:

- destroy inherited equity;
- date quickly;
- reduce fluency;
- increase production burden;
- become the entire concept.

Judge novelty as a trade-off, not a score.

## 6. Category fit vs category mimicry

A brand needs enough category intelligibility to be understood, but category codes should be interrogated.

For each convention ask:

- Does it reduce cognitive/functional friction?
- Does the category genuinely require it?
- Is it so saturated that it erases attribution?
- Can the brand reinterpret it using proprietary assets or behavior?

## 7. Metaphor test

A metaphor is useful when it creates reusable design behavior.

Pass:
- produces multiple applications;
- can operate quietly and expressively;
- does not require explanation;
- survives outside the hero motion piece;
- connects to something true about the brand.

Fail:
- one-to-one illustration of a strategy phrase;
- the system stops working when the metaphor is removed;
- every application repeats the same visual trick;
- competitor could use the metaphor equally well.

## 8. Motion judgment

Evaluate motion through:

- purpose;
- timing;
- continuity with static identity;
- attribution;
- user control;
- accessibility;
- performance;
- fallback;
- ability to scale across touchpoints.

Avoid praising smoothness or technical complexity as ends in themselves.

## 9. Generative-system judgment

A generative identity should expose a controlled relationship between invariants and variables.

Ask:

- What data/randomness/input drives output?
- What prevents visual drift?
- Is the variation meaningful or decorative?
- Can outputs be curated/governed?
- Does the brand remain attributable in low-information conditions?
- Is there a non-generative mode?

## 10. Cultural and semiotic humility

A designer may interpret signs; that does not make the interpretation universal.

When cultural meaning matters:

- specify the cultural context;
- distinguish source-backed interpretation from intuition;
- validate with relevant people/material where consequential;
- avoid importing Western design assumptions into cross-script/place/cultural work.

## 11. Review verdicts

Use three verdicts:

### APPROVE
The mechanism is strategically coherent and system-capable. Remaining work is refinement/prototyping.

### REVISE
The core idea has value but one or more material problems undermine specificity, range, operability, craft, or strategic fit.

### REJECT
The central mechanism is wrong, generic, literal, unscalable, or conflicts with a frozen strategic requirement. Cosmetic fixes will not solve it.

Always say **why** at mechanism level.

## 12. Final question

Before approving a visual territory ask:

> If all presentation rhetoric disappeared and we saw only the system working across ordinary touchpoints, would the idea still be clear, useful, specific and governable?

If not, the presentation is doing work the identity should be doing.
