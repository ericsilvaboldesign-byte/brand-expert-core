# Brand Identity Galleries / Visual Reference Sources

Status: CURATED VISUAL DISCOVERY + OBSERVATION LAYER — NOT PRIMARY EVIDENCE BY DEFAULT

Purpose: strengthen Brand Expert Core with high-volume visual identity, rebranding, packaging and typography reference sources while preserving source authority and avoiding popularity-as-proof.

## Governing distinction

These platforms are valuable for:
- discovering identity systems and rebrands;
- comparative visual research;
- finding before/after material;
- observing typography, packaging, editorial, motion, signage, digital and physical applications;
- finding smaller studios and non-canonical work;
- constructing balanced training and holdout candidate pools.

They do NOT automatically establish:
- the original strategic brief;
- business effectiveness;
- customer memory effects;
- Distinctive Asset fame/uniqueness;
- causal success of a rebrand;
- that a highly liked/project-featured design is strategically strong.

Whenever possible, trace an interesting project back to the client and studio primary sources.

### Identity Designed

Role: SPECIALIST VISUAL-IDENTITY ARCHIVE / PROFESSIONAL DISCOVERY.

The site has operated since 2010. David Airey has described it as a project-sharing archive focused on visual identity work from design studios internationally; his related book was designed to go deeper into studio workflows and identity projects.

Use for:
- visual-identity case discovery;
- studio/process leads;
- finding complete identity systems rather than isolated logos;
- international and smaller-studio sampling.

Boundary:
Editorial/project presentation is not independent evidence of strategic or commercial effectiveness. Trace claims to studio/client sources when possible.

## PRIORITY B — award / packaging / broad professional discovery

### World Brand Design Society (WBDS)

Role: AWARD + PROFESSIONAL COMMUNITY / CONSUMER & CORPORATE BRAND DESIGN DISCOVERY.

WBDS describes itself as a professional community benchmarking and awarding work in corporate and consumer brand design. Current award categories include identity, packaging, graphic, digital, spatial, campaign, illustration, typography, photography and writing.

Use for:
- packaging-led identity systems;
- FMCG and physical-brand applications;
- identity + packaging integration;
- agency, in-house, freelance and student work discovery;
- widening the corpus beyond digital-tech brands.

Boundary:
Award status is an industry recognition signal, not empirical evidence of brand or business effectiveness. Treat project descriptions as entrant/award-platform material unless independently verified.

The site is run by Italian art director/design manager Alessandro Scarpellini, who describes it as a personally researched selection of branding and graphic-design projects. Submissions are reviewed editorially case by case; the site states that it is ad- and sponsorship-free.

Use for:

- smaller international projects that may be absent from large award ecosystems.

Boundary:
Personal editorial curation, not strategy or effectiveness evidence.

### Behance

Role: MASS CREATIVE-PORTFOLIO / DISCOVERY LAYER.

Behance exposes very large visual-identity project pools with filters such as Curated, Most Appreciated, Most Viewed, Most Discussed and Most Recent; Adobe also maintains curated galleries.

Use for:
- finding emerging studios/designers;
- discovering non-canonical and geographically diverse identity projects;
- assembling candidate pools for later source verification;
- observing presentation and execution patterns.

Boundary:
Do not use likes/appreciations/views as quality or effectiveness metrics. Portfolio self-presentation can contain concept work, speculative work, incomplete systems or unverified client claims. Every training case must pass the existing evidence gate independently.

## TYPOGRAPHY-SPECIALIST LAYER

### Fonts In Use

Role: INDEPENDENT TYPOGRAPHY ARCHIVE / OBSERVATION SOURCE.

The platform identifies itself as an independent archive of typography. Its current database contains tens of thousands of documented uses and supports browsing by topic, format and typeface. `Branding/Identity` is a dedicated format category alongside packaging, web, software/apps, signage, posters, editorial and other applications.

Use for:
- identifying exact typefaces used in real identity applications;
- comparing type behavior across categories and media;
- studying type systems beyond logo specimens;
- tracing foundries, designers and application contexts;
- finding current and historical typography references.

Boundary:
Observed use does not prove that a typeface caused brand recognition, differentiation, preference or commercial performance.

CRITICAL CORRECTION:
Do NOT preserve the unsupported claim that “contemporary branding depends 80% on typography.” No credible source has established that percentage in this corpus. Typography can be a major identity component, but its role is brand- and system-specific.

## Working source hierarchy for identity cases

For any case discovered through these galleries, preserve this order:

1. PRIMARY CLIENT / AGENCY SOURCE
2. ACTUAL VISUAL ASSETS / APPLICATIONS
3. SPECIALIST EDITORIAL / CRITICAL COVERAGE
4. AWARD / CURATION PLATFORM
5. COMMUNITY PORTFOLIO / AGGREGATOR

A lower layer can discover or contextualize a case, but it should not silently replace a stronger available source.

## Corpus implication

The training corpus should not be defined by prestige alone. Use these sources to deliberately sample:
- new brands and rebrands;
- evolutionary and radical redesigns;
- B2B and B2C;
- packaging-heavy and digital-heavy systems;
- cultural/institutional identities;
- corporate transformation;
- typography-led systems;
- motion/dynamic systems;
- physical/environmental applications;
- restrained and expressive systems;
- Western and non-Western work;
- large agencies and smaller studios.

The goal is to teach system diagnosis and design judgment, not a house style.
