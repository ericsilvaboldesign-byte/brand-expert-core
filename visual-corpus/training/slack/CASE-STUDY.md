# Slack — Phase 1C Training Case

Status: VERIFIED TRAINING CASE — BOUNDED PARTIAL VISUAL PRIMARY

Learning role: equity preservation + simplification + digital consistency in a B2B/product brand.

Scope boundary: this case is verified for the 2019 identity problem, logo/mark evolution, core palette simplification at the system level, multi-background/logo deployment, app-icon coherence, and later governance/implementation evidence. Typography, extended illustration language, motion execution, physical applications, and the complete 2019 rollout remain incomplete or not directly inspected. Do not generalize beyond that scope.

## 1. BEFORE STATE

### Source-stated condition
Slack's own 2019 launch post says the original identity had a playful, distinctive octothorpe tied directly to the channel symbol used in the product, but it was operationally fragile: eleven colors, a prescribed angle, strong dependence on white backgrounds, and multiple compensating logo variants for different contexts.

Primary source: https://slack.com/intl/en-gb/blog/news/say-hello-new-logo

### Direct visual observation
Primary Slack-hosted visuals were inspected directly.

Observed:
- the pre-2019 mark is a slanted woven/hash-like construction with translucent overlaps and many color intersections;
- examples of incorrect or inconsistent use vary materially in angle, background, lockup treatment and reproduction quality;
- Slack shows three different old app/brand manifestations side by side: the multicolor octothorpe + wordmark, a monochrome hash icon, and a separate multicolor "S" app icon;
- Slack's own collage of older advertising shows very high stylistic variety: illustration, photography, black-and-white cartoon treatment, UI imagery, dimensional lettering, geometric graphics and different compositional idioms. This collage is useful evidence of inconsistency, but it is also a client-curated argument and should not be treated as a neutral sample of every Slack communication.

## 2. BRAND / DESIGN PROBLEM

The evidenced problem was not that Slack lacked recognition or personality. It was that a recognizable identity was difficult to reproduce consistently across backgrounds, scales and digital contexts, which had created a proliferation of substitute marks and weakened cross-touchpoint cohesion.

This matters because the design task was therefore constrained: improve operational robustness without needlessly discarding recognition already attached to the octothorpe and Slack's color world.

## 3. SOURCE-STATED STRATEGIC INTENT

### Slack
Slack frames the change as an evolution rather than change for its own sake. The stated goals were greater simplicity, distinctiveness, cohesion, scalability and recognizability across more contexts while remaining recognizably Slack.

Primary source: https://slack.com/intl/en-gb/blog/news/say-hello-new-logo

### Pentagram
Pentagram states that the team explored multiple directions but ultimately chose to retain the equity of Slack's familiar octothorpe. The mark was rebuilt on a grid from two basic shapes described as a speech bubble and a lozenge. Pentagram also states that the palette was reduced from eleven colors to four primaries, with Slack's aubergine retained as an accent, and that components of the mark were intended to seed icons, illustrations and motifs.

Primary source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/slack

Important boundary: the Pentagram case-page text and captions were accessible, but its image CDN was blocked during this observation pass. Therefore the extended illustration/motif claims are SOURCE-STATED, not directly visually verified here.

## 4. OBSERVED NEW SYSTEM

### Mark and lockup
Directly inspected Slack-hosted images show a compact radial/octothorpe-like symbol paired with a lowercase black wordmark. The symbol is assembled from repeated rounded elements in four colors.

The new system is visibly less dependent on transparency and overlap than the old mark. The color pieces remain separated rather than creating numerous mixed colors at intersections.

### Background behavior
Slack's official launch visual shows the same horizontal logo deployed on white, aubergine and black backgrounds without changing the basic mark construction. This directly supports the stated goal of a mark that survives multiple contexts more reliably.

Primary visual carrier:
- https://d34u8crftukxnk.cloudfront.net/slackpress/prod/sites/6/slack-brand-refresh_03-new-logos.jpg

### Cross-icon coherence
Slack's official comparison of new manifestations shows the same core octothorpe used in the horizontal lockup, as a standalone mark, and inside an app-icon container. Compared with the old three-way mismatch, the relationship between corporate mark and app icon is visibly tighter.

Primary visual carrier:
- https://d34u8crftukxnk.cloudfront.net/slackpress/prod/sites/6/2019-01_BrandRefresh_slack-brand-refresh_04-harmonized-logos.png

### Product/UI evidence
The repo already contains Slack-published platform UI-kit sheets and source files. These establish an inspectable digital/product layer but are not sufficient, by themselves, to prove that the entire 2019 brand graphic language was integrated into product UI.

See `MANIFEST.md`.

## 5. WHAT WAS PRESERVED / WHAT CHANGED

### Preserved
- the octothorpe/hash concept as the primary inherited identifier;
- a four-part multicolor character rather than moving to a monochrome corporate mark;
- lowercase Slack naming in the lockup;
- aubergine as an important contextual brand color;
- a playful rather than austere visual register, according to the source-stated intent.

### Changed
- the old woven/slanted construction became an upright, modular radial construction;
- translucent overlaps and their many resulting color mixtures were removed;
- the palette was rationalized from eleven source-stated colors to four primaries plus aubergine as an accent;
- separate context-specific identity substitutions were replaced by one more consistent family of mark/app-icon manifestations;
- the system became more explicitly modular: mark components could, according to Pentagram, be extracted as a broader graphic vocabulary.

## 6. SYSTEM LOGIC — ANALYST INFERENCE

The core move is best understood as **compression of operational complexity while preserving selected memory structures**.

The redesign did not preserve the old drawing literally. It preserved higher-order cues: the octothorpe idea, multicolor character, lower-case Slack name, product association with channels, and aubergine context. It then simplified the construction so the identity could reproduce across surfaces with fewer exceptional cases.

This is a useful training lesson because "preserve equity" does not necessarily mean "keep the old geometry." It can mean identifying which cues plausibly carry continuity and rebuilding the mechanics around them.

However, this is an inference from the documented and observed case. It is not evidence that every preserved cue was, in fact, a measured distinctive asset with known Fame/Uniqueness scores.

## 7. EXTERNAL CRITIQUE / COUNTER-EVIDENCE

### Brand New / UnderConsideration
Brand New classified the project as a Reviewed before/after case in January 2019. The accessible page in this environment confirms the case and review status but does not expose enough of the substantive review text to quote or summarize its editorial verdict. Use as critique-source locator only for now.

Source: https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_slack_by_pentagram_and_in_house.php

### Daring Fireball
John Gruber argued that the redesign discarded valuable identifiers, specifically the old S, hash and plaid associations, and judged the new mark more generic and less distinctive. This is professional/editorial opinion, not empirical evidence, but it creates an important counter-reading to Pentagram's "retain equity" framing.

Source: https://daringfireball.net/linked/2019/01/16/slack-bland-new-logo

### Design Week
Design Week reported in March 2019 that Slack changed the app icon background from purple to white after launch, with Slack saying the update should improve visibility on devices; some users found the change confusing. This is useful implementation evidence: even a rationalized identity can require post-launch adjustment at a specific touchpoint.

Source: https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/14-20-january-2019/pentagrams-michael-bierut-gives-slack-its-first-ever-rebrand/

## 8. LATER EVIDENCE / GOVERNANCE

Slack's current media kit still directs users to a comprehensive Brand Center covering logo, color, typography, writing and illustration. Current brand-use terms still govern the same octothorpe family and specify clear space using the length of one pill shape, prohibit old logo versions, prohibit rearranging the octothorpe colors, and prohibit changing the relative size/orientation of octothorpe and logotype.

Primary current sources:
- https://slack.com/media-kit
- https://slack.com/intl/en-gb/terms-of-service/slack-brand

This supports continuity and formal governance of the post-2019 core identity. It does **not** prove that the rebrand caused business growth, user preference or greater mental availability.

## 9. WHAT THIS CASE TEACHES

1. A rebrand can start from an implementation failure rather than a positioning failure.
2. Existing equity can be treated as a constraint to reinterpret, not an excuse to preserve every old visual feature.
3. Simplification should be evaluated against system robustness and continuity, not minimalism as an aesthetic virtue.
4. Multi-context digital consistency is observable and testable at the mark/application level.
5. Agency intent and public reception can diverge sharply; both belong in the case record but have different evidentiary status.
6. Post-launch touchpoint corrections are part of the real implementation story and should not be hidden.

## 10. WHAT THIS CASE DOES NOT PROVE

- that the new identity was more distinctive in memory than the old one;
- that retaining the octothorpe preserved measured brand equity;
- that four colors are inherently better than eleven;
- that geometric simplification improves brand effectiveness;
- that the broader illustration, motion or physical system was successful;
- that the rebrand caused any commercial or behavioral outcome.

The case is suitable for bounded training on equity-preserving simplification and digital mark coherence. It is not sufficient as a complete example of every dimension of brand identity.
