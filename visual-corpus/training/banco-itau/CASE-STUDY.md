# Banco Itaú — Phase 1C Training Case

Status: VERIFIED TRAINING CASE — BOUNDED PARTIAL VISUAL PRIMARY

Learning role: Latin American heritage asset evolution + financial brand modernization.

Scope boundary: verified for centenary context, evolution of the rounded-square asset into the `pedra`, logotype changes, orange becoming primary, broader secondary palette, and pedra-based graphic language. Complete motion/product UI and independent effectiveness remain incomplete.

## BEFORE STATE
Itaú entered its centenary with a highly recognizable rounded-square logo that had been associated with the bank since the 1970s, but that shape had become visually less proprietary as rounded app-icon containers became ubiquitous.

## SOURCE-STATED PROBLEM / STRATEGY
Pentagram states that the redesign was intended to draw on Itaú's extraordinary heritage while positioning the bank for the future.

Primary source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/banco-itau

## OBSERVED SYSTEM
Primary Pentagram visuals and additional Brazilian rollout carriers directly inspected show:
- the familiar square evolves into a softer, swollen `pedra` form rather than being abandoned;
- orange becomes the dominant field color;
- white/orange logo applications create strong high-contrast recognition;
- the new shape functions as a container for image and type, a pattern/motif unit and an illustration element;
- broader imagery and illustration language combines photography, large typography and rounded containers.

Primary visual carrier: Pentagram Banco Itaú image results and official case page.
Additional rollout carrier: Brazilian media coverage of Itaú 100.

## PRESERVED / CHANGED
Preserved: name, basic container/logo ancestry, orange equity and the central recognizer relationship.
Changed: geometry becomes more proprietary/organic; orange moves to primary; logotype geometry is softened; the logo shape expands into a full graphic language.

## SYSTEM LOGIC
The case demonstrates asset evolution rather than asset replacement. The same recognizer is pushed far enough to regain ownability and supply a broader compositional grammar, while continuity remains legible.

## CRITIQUE / LIMITS
The claim that the old shape was too ubiquitous is a strategic/design judgment rather than measured loss of Fame or Uniqueness. The visual case alone cannot establish whether the new `pedra` has already accumulated distinctive-asset strength in memory.

Training boundary: use this case to reason about how far a known asset can evolve while preserving continuity; do not call the pedra empirically distinctive without memory-structure evidence.
