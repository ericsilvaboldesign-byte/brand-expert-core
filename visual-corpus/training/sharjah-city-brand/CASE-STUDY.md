# Sharjah City Brand — Phase 1C Training Case

Status: VERIFIED TRAINING CASE — BOUNDED PARTIAL VISUAL PRIMARY

Learning role: place branding + bilingual identity + cross-script parity + motion/icon system.

Scope boundary: verified for strategy, bilingual wordmark logic, arch device, orange/secondary palette, motion/icon behavior and equal-status English/Arabic treatment. Full city rollout, environmental system and outcome evidence remain incomplete.

## BEFORE STATE
Sharjah had strong cultural, educational, industrial and historical assets but needed a clearer city identity that could connect heritage with a more ambitious arts/culture and future-facing narrative for residents, visitors and business audiences.

## SOURCE-STATED PROBLEM / STRATEGY
Pentagram states that the identity was designed to connect Sharjah's rich history to its ambitious cultural scene and promote the emirate as a place to live, work and visit. The revived `Smile, you're in Sharjah` line supplies a friendly tonal anchor.

Primary source: https://www.pentagram.com/work/sharjah-city-brand

## OBSERVED SYSTEM
Primary Pentagram visuals directly inspected show:
- an arch motif functioning as both letterform intervention and standalone icon/container;
- vibrant orange as the principal color with green, yellow, purple and earthier supporting tones;
- English and Arabic wordmarks presented with matched visual authority rather than a primary/secondary hierarchy;
- the arch embedded differently in each script instead of mechanically forcing identical shapes;
- social cards, photography, icons and repeated wordmarks using the same rounded geometry;
- icon/motion logic based on the arch opening, transforming and revealing content.

Primary visual carriers: Pentagram Sharjah case imagery and story page.

## PRESERVED / CHANGED
Preserved: local architectural reference, bilingual reality, heritage/cultural context and the remembered 1970s welcome phrase.
Changed: city expression becomes systematized through one cross-script grammar, dedicated palette and motion/icon toolkit.

## SYSTEM LOGIC
The key lesson is parity without mimicry. The two scripts share proportions, stroke logic, color and behavior while each wordmark places the arch where it naturally belongs. The identity creates family resemblance through design DNA rather than translating one script's geometry literally into the other.

## CRITIQUE / LIMITS
The arch carries multiple source-stated meanings—architecture, passage, transformation, journey. Those meanings are project rationale, not universal semiotic facts. No independent effectiveness evidence was identified.

Training boundary: use this case for multilingual/cross-script system reasoning and cultural sensitivity, not as a general rule that visual parity requires identical dimensions in every bilingual identity.
